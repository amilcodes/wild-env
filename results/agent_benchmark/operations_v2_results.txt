WILD-ENV OPERATIONS V2 — REAL MODEL EVALUATION

Scope: synthetic sequential response planning with deadlines, prerequisites,
shared water, nonpreemptive teams, and information acquisition under a hidden forecast.
Models use a structured dispatch interface; browser, shell, and external tools are disallowed.
This is a separate operational abstraction. It does not establish wildfire realism.
The original fire simulator and v1.1 evidence remain available unchanged.

Evaluation: 12 generated instances across six known families,
two size/complexity settings, both forecast outcomes where relevant, and two model repetitions.
306 episodes; 2601 dispatches; 1836 real model calls.
Development (excluded from rankings): 44 episodes, 216 model calls.

Primary score: cumulative exact Bellman regret / initial information-matched
optimal expected reward, multiplied by 100. Lower is better; zero is optimal
and holding throughout scores 100. Values are not clipped. Each instance has
equal weight after collapsing worlds and repetitions; the independent sample size is 12.

VALIDATION AND REUSE
All episode traces replayed; checksums, effective prompts, model identities, and
identical initial observations across policies/worlds/repetitions verified.
No disallowed tool events or infrastructure errors. Invalid model actions remain scored.
Every initial full-information optimum passed an independent MIP check.
An independent solver using public packets checked 1899 evaluation decision penalties
across 775 unique reached states. Maximum numeric disagreement: 5.68e-14.
Conditional pre-revelation decisions use the information-matched Bellman reference
and analytical information tests; the fully informed MIP cannot certify those decisions.
Repository tests: 218 passed, one skipped. Lint and CI passed.
The archive includes task generation configuration, public decision traces, exact
per-decision feedback, verified development demonstrations, provenance, and sensitivity checks.

LIMITS
Held-out seeds within six known synthetic families; no demonstrated training gain,
cross-domain transfer, expert-calibrated wildfire loss, or broad frontier ranking.
All models requested high effort, with equal decision opportunities. Provider CLI
scaffolding differs, effort is not compute-matched, and output tokens are not capped.
Claude effort is requested rather than independently attested by CLI events.
Repeated calls and forecast worlds are not counted as additional independent tasks.
